    Khoeli · The Moon

    Planting by the light ea khoeli

    Traditional practice ties fieldwork to the moon's cycle. Offered here as cultural learning to be read alongside the rainfall calendar.

    New moon

    Khoeli e ncha

    The dark nights. The cycle's quiet beginning — rest the fields, mend tools, and turn the compost before sowing resumes with the returning light.

    Waxing moon

    Khoeli e ntseng e hōla

    The growing light. The traditional window for sowing seed and transplanting seedlings, as the month builds toward fullness.

    Full moon

    Khoeli e tletseng

    The bright nights. A time for gathering, community work, and checking fields — the veld is lit long after sunset.

    Waning moon

    Khoeli e fokotsehang

    The fading light. The traditional window for weeding, pruning, and harvesting for storage, as the month draws down.

       

    Litaba tsa setso: moon-phase planting is presented as indigenous practice and cultural heritage. Pair it with the seasonal rainfall guidance above, which remains the primary planting framework.
